How to play

Tap "Start the game" and the game shows you one riddle at a time — first in Amharic, with the English underneath. Read it, think it over, and say your answer out loud before you reveal it. Feeling brave? Run the 5-second countdown and beat the clock. After the reveal, see if you cracked it — there are no wrong answers here, only riddles you haven't cracked yet.

Play again and the riddles come in a fresh order, so the game never plays the same way twice. It is a lovely game for the whole family: grandparents know the old riddles by heart, and kids love the "aha!" moment when the answer clicks.

Tips to play well

  • Listen for the wordplay. Most riddles turn on one word with two meanings — find the word that could mean something else, and the answer is usually hiding behind it.
  • Picture it literally. "Hands that cannot clap" is a clue to take literally: what in your house has hands but never claps?
  • Say your answer out loud before revealing. It makes the game honest — and the groans and cheers are half the fun.
  • Play it as a race. With a group, the first person to shout the right answer takes the point. First to 5 wins the round.

Why it's fun

እንቆቅልሽ — riddles — are one of the oldest games in Ethiopian culture: a test of wit passed down at family gatherings, around the buna ceremony, and on long car rides. These riddles are for everyone — no special knowledge needed, just a sharp mind and a sense of humour. The injera riddle is a favourite: a thousand eyes, and it still cannot see!
